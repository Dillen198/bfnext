# Red Storm Rising: research notes

What the scenario is built from: the novel's campaign (part 1) and what DCS
World can field for it as of October 2026 (part 2). The design that turns this
into Fowl Engine campaigns is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

Everything about the book is paraphrased; nothing is quoted. The book never
names its year: its hardware is 1985-86. Items marked **[uncertain]** rest on
one weak source (a forum recollection, a machine-written chapter summary) and
should be checked against the book before they become campaign canon.

---

## Part 1: the novel as a military campaign

### 1.1 Why the war starts

1. **Nizhnevartovsk.** Three Azerbaijani saboteurs led by an engineer at the
   plant wreck the western Siberian oil complex. Soviet crude output drops by
   about a third for two to three years.
2. **The decision.** The Politburo will not go cap in hand to the West. It
   decides to take the **Persian Gulf** oilfields, and because the United
   States would fight for the Gulf, NATO has to be knocked out first: a fast
   conventional offensive into West Germany ("Red Storm"), with about four
   months to prepare.
3. **Maskirovka.** To get strategic surprise:
   - an arms-control offer (halve nuclear arsenals) and the scrapping of old
     missile submarines, to lull the West;
   - a KGB bomb in the Kremlin that kills a group of visiting children, blamed
     on a planted "West German agent", to make the war look like self-defence;
   - a Spetsnaz plan to hit NATO's communications hub at Lammersdorf near
     Aachen. It leaks when the Spetsnaz officer carrying the plan is hit by a
     car, which gives NATO a few days' warning.
4. **The sea plan.** NATO lives on reinforcement by sea. Taking **Iceland**
   breaks the GIUK gap (SOSUS and maritime-patrol barrier), gives the Soviets
   a fighter base, and lets Naval Aviation bombers reach the convoy lanes.
   Soviet missile submarines stay in their **Barents Sea bastion**, which frees
   the attack submarines for the Atlantic.

**The Gulf operation never happens.** The war in Europe goes wrong before the
Soviets can turn south.

### 1.3 Theatres

#### Central Front (West Germany)

- **Opening night ("Dreamland").**
  - The fictional F-19 "Frisbee" stealth fighter flies low through the SAM
    belts and kills the A-50 Mainstay radar planes.
  - Massed strikes follow on Pact airfields, air defences and the Elbe
    bridges, catching much of the Soviet second echelon short of the river.
  - The Soviets answer with a mass MiG-23 raid (about 48 aircraft) that drives
    off NATO's E-3s and briefly wins local air superiority.
- **The ground war.**
  - Soviet tank armies push into the North German Plain. NATO trades ground
    for losses and burns through its ammunition.
  - The Soviet commander (Alekseyev) runs the front from **Stendal**.
- **Alfeld on the Leine.**
  - Four motor-rifle divisions and a tank division attack German, British and
    US defenders.
  - NATO artillery drops the Leine bridges. The Soviets force a crossing
    anyway and build the bridgehead up to division strength.
- **Toward the Weser.**
  - Operational Maneuver Groups are committed toward **Hameln**.
  - A Belgian anti-tank counterattack stops them.
- **The fuel war.**
  - NATO traces Soviet supply trucks back to their fuel and ammunition dumps,
    using radar data played backward.
  - Once NATO learns the war is about oil, it puts its air power on Soviet
    **fuel**: dumps and tanker-truck columns, hit at night by the stealth
    fighters from **Bitburg**.
- **Endgame.**
  - The Soviets attack again around Fölziehausen and push toward the Weser
    and the Ruhr, short of fuel.
  - NATO masses an armoured counterstroke at Alfeld.
  - Both sides are exhausted at the ceasefire.
- **Not described in the book:** Hamburg, Lüneburg, Hannover and the Fulda
  Gap do not appear as named battles. They are front-line geography only.

#### Iceland

- **Soviet seizure.**
  - Missile and bomber strikes wreck NAS Keflavík and its F-15 detachment.
  - The barge carrier *Julius Fučík*, posing as a merchant ship, lands naval
    infantry with hovercraft and barges. She is hit by a P-3's Harpoon on the
    way in but still reaches port.
  - An airborne division arrives by airlift.
  - A MiG-29 regiment and SAMs move into Keflavík.
- **The stay-behind team.**
  - A USAF weather officer and a few Marines escape into the lava country.
  - They report Soviet positions by satellite radio, fight a Soviet patrol,
    and are joined by Royal Marines dropped by parachute.
  - They scout **Stykkishólmur**, survive a final mortar and infantry attack
    with US close air support, and are flown out.
- **NATO's air campaign against Keflavík.**
  1. 18 B-52s with an EF-111 and 4 F-4Gs against 8 MiG-29s on CAP. 3 of 5
     runway surfaces cut, about half the bombers lost.
  2. A low-level F-111 raid on the runways and the power station.
  3. A jamming B-52 force lures up 26 MiG-29s into an F-14 ambush, which kills
     21 of them.
  4. Final carrier strike: A-6s with anti-radiation missiles, F-14 cover and a
     Hornet sweep.
- **Recapture.**
  - Force Recon silences the Stykkishólmur outpost.
  - Marines land with tanks, supported by battleship gunfire (*Iowa*, *New
    Jersey*) and Harriers.
  - The Soviet commander surrenders.

#### North Atlantic and the GIUK gap

- **Dance of the Vampires.**
  - Badgers fire AS-5 missiles fitted to look like Backfires on radar. The
    F-14s spend their Phoenixes on the decoys.
  - Then waves of Tu-22M Backfires launch their missiles. Only *Foch*'s F-8s
    are in position to intercept.
  - *Foch* and *Saipan* (about 2,000 Marines) are sunk. *Nimitz* and
    *Saratoga* are damaged and spend weeks in British drydock.
- **The convoy war.**
  - Daily Backfire and Badger raids from Kola sink about 60 merchant ships in
    the first weeks.
  - Frigate actions: the Knox-class *Pharris* kills a Charlie-class submarine
    and is then torpedoed by a Victor. The Perry-class *Reuben James* and HMS
    *Battleaxe* form an ASW hunting pair.
  - A mid-ocean ambush at 31° West **[details uncertain]**.
- **The air war over the Atlantic and northern Britain.**
  - F-14s from the damaged *Nimitz* fly from RAF Stornoway and hunt Backfire
    tankers.
  - A Tu-22 Blinder raid hits Stornoway.
  - An F-15C pilot on a ferry flight kills three returning Badgers, and later
    fires ASAT missiles at Soviet radar satellites.
- **Operation Doolittle.**
  1. A bait convoy draws the Backfires, and Tomcats kill their Bear scouts.
  2. US submarines fire Tomahawks timed to hit **four Kola airfields** just
     after the bombers land and are refuelling in the open.
  3. The damaged US submarines then limp out ("Race of the Cripples").

#### Norway and Kola

- Soviet air attacks wear down the Norwegian Air Force early.
- A Soviet surface group sails toward **Bodø**. The coastal submarine HNoMS
  *Kobben* sinks **Kirov** north of **Narvik**, and USS *Chicago*'s Harpoons
  cover her escape.
- Kola is the bomber, fleet and bastion base area (Severomorsk, Olenya, the
  Murmansk fields), and Doolittle's target.
- Which Norwegian airfields the Soviets take is not described
  **[uncertain]**.

#### Elsewhere

- The southern flank, Japan and Greece stay out, and the Pacific is quiet.
- Pacific carriers move to the Atlantic.
- Satellites are fought on both sides: Soviet blinding, US F-15 ASAT shots.

### 1.5 Set pieces

Each one is listed with its forces and what each side is trying to do.

1. **Dreamland.**
   - Forces: 2-6 stealth strikers against 2-3 A-50s behind the inner-German
     border, then bridge strikes on the Elbe.
   - Aims: kill the radar planes, drop the bridges.
2. **AWACS counter-raid.**
   - Forces: about 40-48 MiG-23s against a pair of E-3s with F-15 and F-16
     CAP.
   - Aims: Red kills the E-3s; Blue protects them.
3. **Polar Glory.**
   - Forces: bomber and missile strike on the island airbase; an assault ship
     running in; a transport stream with MiG-29 escort. Against them, 2-4 F-15s
     and a P-3 with Harpoon.
   - Aims: Blue sinks the ship and kills the transports; Red gets them in.
4. **Dance of the Vampires.**
   - Forces: decoy missiles first, then 20 or more Tu-22M3 against a carrier
     group with an LHA.
   - Aims: protect the LHA and the carriers.
5. **Nordic Hammer.**
   - Forces: 18 B-52s with jammer and Wild Weasel support against 8 MiG-29s
     on CAP and the SAMs.
   - Aims: cut 3 runway surfaces.
6. **Low-level raid** on the island's runways and power station.
7. **MiG-29 trap.**
   - Forces: jammer bait and an F-14 ambush against 26 MiG-29s.
8. **Blinders on Stornoway.**
   - Aims: defend the airfield and shoot down the missiles.
9. **Tanker hunt** north of the Faroes.
10. **Ferry-flight ambush.**
    - Forces: 4 F-15Cs, each with one AIM-9 and guns, against 12 returning
      bombers.
11. **Convoy escort and ASW.**
    - Forces: frigates and a LAMPS helicopter against submarines, with bomber
      raids on the convoy.
12. **Kirov off Narvik.**
    - Aims: a maritime strike on a surface action group.
13. **Doolittle.**
    - Phase 1: ambush the bombers over the bait convoy.
    - Phase 2: a timed Tomahawk strike on four Kola airfields while the
      bombers are on the ground.
14. **Final strike on the island.**
    - Forces: anti-radiation strike and a fighter sweep against the SAM ring.
15. **The landing at Stykkishólmur.**
    - Sequence: recon insertion, silence the outpost, amphibious landing with
      gunfire support, then defend the recon team's hilltop and extract it.
16. **Alfeld breakthrough.**
    - Forces: 4 motor-rifle divisions and 1 tank division, with Mi-24s and
      Su-25s, against German, British and US troops, A-10s and AH-64s.
    - Aims: hold or take the Leine bridges.
17. **Fuel war.**
    - Aims: find and destroy fuel dumps and tanker columns, at night.
18. **The commander's helicopter.**
    - Forces: an F-4 sweep catches a Mi-8 VIP flight escorted by MiGs.
19. **Final battle.**
    - Forces: a NATO armoured counterstroke against a fuel-starved Soviet
      salient short of the Weser.
    - Aims: settle the line before the ceasefire.

### 2.5 Submarines and anti-ship warfare in DCS

**Submarines and ASW**

- Submarines can be placed submerged, but there is **no sonar model**. They
  are found only on the surface or at periscope depth.
- ASW weapons don't work: no sonobuoys, dipping sonar, MAD or SOSUS.
- AI submarines don't attack: no torpedo tubes are defined on Kilo or Tango
  **[forum, uncertain]**.
- ASW therefore has to be **scripted**. The freeware "DCS ASW Operation"
  framework shows a way to do it.

**Anti-ship missiles**

- **Soviet:** Kh-22 on the Tu-22M3, P-700 Granit, P-500 Bazalt, Moskit, Kh-35,
  Kh-31A, Kh-59.
- **NATO:** Harpoon, Exocet, RB-04 and RB-15.
- **Tomahawk** is land-attack only (no TASM).
- AI Tu-22M3s need "expend all" and a group attack to fire a full salvo
  **[forum]**.
- Ships do shoot down missiles, and saturation can work. Test this in the
  current build.
